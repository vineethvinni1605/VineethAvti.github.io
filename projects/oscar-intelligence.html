01

THE PROBLEM

What signals are associated with an Oscar-winning film?

Academy Award outcomes reflect a complex combination of audience engagement, financial performance, genre, release timing and other characteristics. This project investigated whether those signals could be transformed into measurable features for machine learning analysis.

The objective was not only to classify potential winners, but also to understand which characteristics carried the strongest predictive signal, how those relationships changed over time and whether patterns differed across major award categories.

02

THE DATA

Combining large-scale movie data with decades of Oscar history.

The project combined TMDB movie metadata with historical Academy Award records covering 1927–2025. After cleaning, title standardization, duplicate removal and integration, the analytical dataset contained 972,435 film records across 30 fields.

A total of 859 Oscar-winning films were identified. After filtering records with missing financial, engagement or category information, 324 complete records were retained for the modeling analysis.

FEATURE ENGINEERING

Turning raw movie metadata into interpretable predictive signals.

01

Financial

Budget, revenue and return on investment (ROI).

02

Audience

Vote average, vote count, popularity and log-transformed engagement measures.

03

Context

Main genre, award category, release year and release decade.

04

Text

Sentiment polarity derived from movie overview text.

Engineered features included ROI, log popularity, log vote count, release decade, simplified main genre, sentiment score and a binary winner indicator.

04

EXPLORATORY ANALYSIS

Understanding the structure behind the data.

Exploratory analysis was used to examine relationships between financial performance, audience engagement, popularity and other numerical variables before modeling.

01 / CORRELATION ANALYSIS

Vote count and popularity showed a strong positive relationship, while budget and revenue were also strongly associated. ROI and sentiment showed much weaker linear relationships with the other numerical variables.

02 / FINANCIAL LANDSCAPE

Budget and revenue varied substantially across Oscar-winning films. The visualization also reveals high-revenue outliers and variation across genres, illustrating why financial scale alone cannot explain award outcomes.

04

MODELING

Four models. One comparative framework.

MODEL 01

Logistic Regression

Interpretable linear baseline for classification and probability estimation.

MODEL 02

Random Forest

Ensemble approach for modeling nonlinear relationships and examining feature importance.

MODEL 03

Support Vector Machine

RBF-based classification approach for identifying complex decision boundaries.

MODEL 04

Gradient Boosting

Delivered the strongest overall performance, reaching approximately 75% accuracy and 75% F1-score.

Model evaluation used accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score and five-fold cross-validation. Because Oscar winners represented only about 0.09% of the overall film data, class weights, category filtering and SMOTE were used to address severe class imbalance.

MODEL INTERPRETABILITY

What did the Random Forest pay attention to?

Feature importance was examined to understand how individual models used the available signals. In the Random Forest model, financial variables ranked highly alongside audience-engagement features such as vote count, vote average and popularity.

03 / RANDOM FOREST FEATURE IMPORTANCE

Revenue and budget received the highest importance within this Random Forest model, followed by vote count, vote average and popularity. This model-specific view complements the broader feature analysis conducted across the project.

KEY FINDINGS

Audience engagement emerged as the strongest signal.

01

Vote count led the model.

Vote count emerged as the strongest overall predictor, reflecting long-term audience engagement and film visibility.

02

Popularity and genre mattered.

Popularity and main genre carried meaningful predictive information, with Documentary and Animation showing particularly strong patterns.

03

Financial scale wasn't everything.

ROI contributed to prediction but played a smaller role than audience-engagement measures such as vote count and popularity.

04

Time changed the pattern.

Release decade captured shifts in winning patterns across different periods of Academy Award history.

05

Text added incremental signal.

Sentiment derived from movie overviews provided a smaller but useful contribution alongside numerical and categorical features.

07

MODEL PERFORMANCE

Ensemble methods captured the complexity most effectively.

Gradient Boosting achieved the strongest overall performance at approximately 75% accuracy and 75% F1-score. Random Forest also demonstrated strong and stable performance.

Logistic Regression and Support Vector Machine provided useful comparison baselines but were less effective at capturing the nonlinear relationships present across financial, audience, temporal and categorical features.

Cross-validation results were also examined separately to evaluate model stability and generalization rather than relying on a single performance measure.

04 / CLASSIFICATION PERFORMANCE

Confusion matrices provided a more detailed view of classification behavior by showing correct predictions alongside false positives and false negatives.

LIMITATIONS

Prediction cannot capture every dimension of creative success.

The analysis was constrained by missing financial information for older films, inconsistent genre labels, limited samples in some award categories and the limitations of sentiment derived from short movie descriptions.

The models identify statistical patterns rather than deterministic rules. Artistic judgment and other qualitative influences on Academy Award outcomes are not fully represented by the available features.

10

NEXT STEPS

Extending the system beyond the initial modeling framework.

Future iterations could incorporate critic-review datasets, richer NLP features such as topic modeling and emotion detection, and a prediction API for evaluating new movie inputs.

These additions could provide richer textual context while creating a more interactive system for exploring the relationship between film characteristics and historical award outcomes.
