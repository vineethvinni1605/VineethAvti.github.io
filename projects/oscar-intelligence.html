01

THE PROBLEM

What signals are associated with an Oscar-winning film?

Award outcomes are influenced by a complex combination of audience reception, financial performance, genre, timing and other factors. This project explored whether those signals could be transformed into measurable features for machine learning analysis.

The goal was not simply to build a classifier, but to understand which characteristics were most informative and whether those relationships changed across time and award categories.

02

THE DATA

Combining nearly a million film records with Oscar history.

The analytical dataset combined movie metadata with historical Academy Award information. After cleaning and integration, the dataset contained approximately 972,435 films and 30 analytical fields.

972,435 CLEANED FILMS
859 IDENTIFIED WINNERS

MODELING

Four models. One comparative framework.

MODEL 01

Logistic Regression

Baseline linear classification model.

MODEL 02

Random Forest

Ensemble model for nonlinear feature relationships.

MODEL 03

Support Vector Machine

RBF-based classification approach.

MODEL 04

Gradient Boosting

Strongest overall model in the comparative evaluation.

Evaluation included five-fold cross-validation, class-imbalance handling and comparison across accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score.

05

RESULTS

The strongest signals weren't necessarily the obvious ones.

01

Audience engagement mattered.

Vote count emerged as one of the strongest predictors, with popularity also contributing meaningful signal.

02

Genre carried information.

Documentary and animation-related categories showed notable predictive relationships.

03

Financial scale wasn't everything.

ROI contributed to the models but played a smaller role than audience engagement signals.

04

Text added incremental signal.

Sentiment derived from movie descriptions provided a smaller but useful additional feature.
